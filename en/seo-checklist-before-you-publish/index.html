The SEO checklist to run before you publish a page

A short list to run through before you press publish. None of it is magic. Together, it removes the common mistakes that stop a good page from being found or understood.

By Paul Alain Nyoum2 November 20262 min read

This article is also available in French: Lire la version française.

On this page

  • The page itself
  • Content and images
  • Technical basics
  • After publishing

Tick these off page by page. Every page on this blog follows the list, and you can view the source of any page to see how.

The page itself#

  • One clear topic. You can say what the page is about in one sentence.
  • A title tag that matches the topic, around 60 characters or fewer, and different from every other page on your site.
  • A meta description of about 150 characters that describes the page honestly. It is not a direct ranking factor, but it is often the text shown under your title, so it affects whether people click.
  • One h1, then h2 and h3 in order, without skipping levels for styling.
  • A short, readable URL: lower case, words separated by hyphens, no dates or random numbers.

Content and images#

  • The answer to the main question appears early.
  • Every image that carries meaning has alt text that describes it. Purely decorative images get an empty alt.
  • Links use descriptive text, never click here.
  • The page links to one or two related pages on your own site.
  • Facts, prices and dates are correct, and sources are named.

Technical basics#

  • Mobile friendly: text readable without zooming, no sideways scrolling, buttons easy to tap.
  • Fast enough: compress images, set their width and height to avoid layout jumps, and load only the fonts you use.
  • A canonical link points to the preferred URL of the page.
  • The page is listed in your sitemap.xml and is not blocked by robots.txt.
  • Social tags (og:title, og:description, og:image) are set, so shared links look right.
  • Structured data such as BlogPosting passes a testing tool.
  • On a multilingual site, each language version has an hreflang tag pointing to the others and to itself.

After publishing#

  • Open the live page on your phone and read it once.
  • Submit the URL or the sitemap in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
  • Share the page where your readers are, and link to it from related older posts.
  • Come back after a few weeks. Which searches brought people here? Improve the page accordingly.
In shortPages are rarely found because of one trick. They are found because many small things are right at the same time.
