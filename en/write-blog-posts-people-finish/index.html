How to write blog posts that people finish

Most blog posts do not fail because the writing is bad. They fail because nobody decided who the post was for, or which question it answers.

By Paul Alain Nyoum2 November 20263 min read

On this page

  • Start with the question, not the keyword
  • Put the answer where people can see it
  • Write a title that promises one thing
  • Use headings as an outline
  • Keep sentences short and words plain
  • Link to your own pages with clear words
  • End with one next step

Here is the routine I follow before and while writing, in the order I do it. It works for a company blog, a portfolio, or a newsletter archive.

Start with the question, not the keyword#

A keyword is only the words someone types. Behind it sits a question or a task: how do I…, which one should I choose, what does this mean. Write that question down in one sentence before you write anything else. This is what people call search intent.

Then look at what already ranks. Type your phrase into a search engine and read the first page. Are the results step-by-step guides, short definitions, comparison lists or product pages? That tells you what searchers expect to find. If every result is a how-to guide and you publish a personal essay, you are asking the reader to change their mind before they have read a word.

Put the answer where people can see it#

Give the short answer in the first two or three sentences, then use the rest of the post to explain, show examples and cover exceptions. Readers on a phone decide in seconds whether to stay. A clear opening also helps search engines understand what the page is about.

Write a title that promises one thing#

  • Say what the reader gets. 7 translation traps on websites beats Translation tips.
  • Keep it near 60 characters so it is less likely to be cut off in search results. Search engines cut by pixel width, not by character count, so treat this as a guide, not a rule.
  • Put the main phrase early, in a natural way. Never repeat it just to fit it in.

Do not trick readers. A title that over-promises earns the click and then the quick exit, which helps nobody.

Use headings as an outline#

Each page should have one h1, which is the title, and h2 headings for the main parts. If someone read only your headings, they should still follow the argument. Headings also let screen reader users jump through a page, so this is accessibility and SEO at the same time.

Avoid vague headings like More information. What to check on mobile tells the reader what they will get.

Keep sentences short and words plain#

Aim for paragraphs of two to four lines on a phone. Cut words that carry no meaning: in order to becomes to, and due to the fact that becomes because. Read the draft out loud, and split any sentence that makes you run out of breath.

Link to your own pages with clear words#

Internal links help readers find the next useful page and help search engines discover and connect your content. Use anchor text that describes the target: link the pre-publishing SEO checklist, not click here. Add links where they truly help the reader, not in every sentence.

End with one next step#

Close by telling the reader what to do next: read a related post, use a checklist, or get in touch. One clear step beats five weak ones.

In shortDecide the question. Answer it early. Keep the title and headings honest. Write plainly. Link to what helps next. Nobody can promise you the first page of results, but these habits make a page clearer for people, and that is what search engines try to reward.
