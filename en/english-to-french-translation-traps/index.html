7 English to French translation traps on websites

Translating a website is not swapping words. These are the seven mistakes I check for first, with examples you can test yourself.

1. False friends that change the meaning#

Some words look the same in English and French but mean different things. They are the most common reason a translated page sounds almost right and is still wrong.

Six false friends to watch for

If a sentence reads suspiciously smoothly, check each word against a good dictionary.

2. Punctuation: spaces and quotes#

In France, a non-breaking space goes before ?, !, : and ;, and French quotation marks look like « this », with spaces inside. In HTML, write &nbsp; so the mark never ends up alone at the start of a line. Canadian French handles some of these marks differently, so know which audience you are writing for.

3. Numbers, dates, times and money#

  • French uses a decimal comma and a space for thousands: 1 234,56, where English writes 1,234.56.
  • The day comes first: 02/11/2026 means 2 November, not 11 February.
  • The 24-hour clock is the norm: 14 h 30 or 14:30.
  • The currency follows the number, after a space: 25 €.

An error here can mean a wrong price or a missed appointment, so check every figure twice.

4. Buttons and calls to action#

Do not translate button labels word by word. Sign up becomes S’inscrire, Get started becomes Commencer, and Learn more becomes En savoir plus. French text is often noticeably longer than English, so test buttons and menus with the real words, not placeholder text.

5. “Tu” or “vous”#

Decide once and keep to it. Most business and service websites in France use vous, which is polite and safe. Some young brands and apps use tu on purpose. Mixing the two on one site looks careless.

6. Capital letters#

English headings often use Title Case. French normally capitalises only the first word and proper names. Days, months and languages are written in lower case (lundi, novembre, français), and so are adjectives of nationality.

7. Keywords: translate how people search#

A literal translation of your English keyword may not be what French speakers type. Free trial is commonly searched as essai gratuit. Search the French phrase yourself, read the results and the suggestions, and use the words that real pages use.

Then tell search engines which page is which language with hreflang tags. The SEO checklist covers this, and this blog does it for the French version of that checklist.

In shortGood translation checks meaning, format, tone and search habits, not only vocabulary. Whatever the tool or the translator, have a native speaker read the final page.
